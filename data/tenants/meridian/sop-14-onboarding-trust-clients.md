# SOP 14 — Onboarding a trust client

> SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT — fictional firm, written for a product demo.

Owner: AML/CTF Compliance Officer · Version 2.2 · Effective 1 July 2026

## Purpose

Trusts are the highest-volume source of CDD errors at Meridian. This procedure
sets out exactly who must be identified when the customer is a trust.

## Who the customer is

The customer is the trustee acting in its capacity as trustee. But identifying
the trustee alone is not sufficient — you must also identify the people behind
the trust.

## Step 1 — Obtain the trust deed

Obtain a complete copy of the trust deed and any deeds of variation. A summary,
a stamped cover page, or the accountant's file note is not acceptable.

Record: the full name of the trust, the type of trust (discretionary, unit,
testamentary, SMSF), the date of establishment, and the country of
establishment.

## Step 2 — Identify the trustee

If the trustee is an individual, apply SOP 03.

If the trustee is a company, apply SOP 04 to the company and identify each
director.

## Step 3 — Identify the beneficial owners

This is the step most often done incompletely. For a trust you must identify:

- **The settlor**, unless the trust was established by will, or the initial
  settlement sum was under $10,000 and the settlor has no ongoing role
- **Each beneficiary** who is named in the deed
- **For a discretionary trust with a class of beneficiaries**, the class as
  described in the deed, plus each individual who has actually received a
  distribution in the last two financial years — check the distribution
  minutes, do not rely on the deed alone
- **The appointor or principal**, being any person who can remove and replace
  the trustee. This person controls the trust in substance and is frequently
  missed
- **Any protector or guardian** named in the deed

## Step 4 — Verify

Verify the identity of each individual identified in Step 3 using SOP 03.

Where a beneficial owner is an entity rather than an individual, work through
the ownership chain until you reach the individuals who ultimately own or
control it.

## Step 5 — Risk rate

Trust clients start at MEDIUM risk at Meridian.

Escalate to HIGH where any of the following apply:

- The trust was established in, or has a trustee or beneficial owner resident
  in, a jurisdiction on the elevated-risk list
- The appointor is not an obvious family member or professional adviser
- The trust structure involves two or more layers of interposed entities
- The deed has been varied more than twice
- The client is reluctant to identify the appointor

## Step 6 — Approval

All trust onboarding is reviewed by the Compliance Officer before the
engagement proceeds. Partners do not have authority to waive this review.

## Common errors

- Identifying the corporate trustee but not its directors
- Treating the deed's list of potential beneficiaries as sufficient and not
  checking actual distributions
- Missing the appointor entirely
- Accepting an unsigned or undated deed
- Onboarding the trust and then adding the beneficial owners "later"
